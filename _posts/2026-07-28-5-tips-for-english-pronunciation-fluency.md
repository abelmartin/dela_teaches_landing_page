Speaking English with clarity and confidence doesn't mean removing your accent—it means learning how to produce sounds clearly so listeners understand you effortlessly on the first try.

Here are 5 practical strategies you can start applying today in your daily practice:

## 1. Pay Attention to Ending Consonants
Many English learners naturally drop or swallow the final sounds of words (such as the *d*, *t*, *s*, or *ed* endings). For instance, saying "pas" instead of "past" or "work" instead of "worked". 

> **Practice Tip:** Read aloud for 5 minutes every day. Focus specifically on enunciating every final consonant clearly.

## 2. Master Word and Sentence Stress
English is a **time-stressed language**, meaning rhythm comes from emphasizing key content words (nouns, main verbs, adjectives) while blending structure words (prepositions, articles).

When you stress the right syllables in a word:
- *PRE-sent* (noun) vs. *pre-SENT* (verb)
- *pho-to-graph* vs. *pho-TO-gra-pher*

Your speech immediately sounds more natural to native speakers.

## 3. Read Out Loud
Reading quietly in your mind builds vocabulary, but reading out loud builds **muscle memory** in your mouth, tongue, and jaw. 

Find a short news article, book excerpt, or essay. Read it slowly twice, then read it a third time at normal conversational speed.

## 4. Shadowing Technique
Shadowing is listening to an English speaker (such as a podcast host, audiobook narrator, or news anchor) and repeating what they say with just a 1-second delay.

This helps you match their intonation, pausing, and rhythm in real time.

## 5. Record Yourself and Listen Back
It can feel uncomfortable at first, but recording a 1-minute voice note on your phone is one of the fastest ways to spot areas for improvement. Compare your recording to native audio and notice where your rhythm or pronunciation differs.
